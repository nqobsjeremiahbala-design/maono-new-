Open any forex statistic and the same number comes up: roughly 70 to 90 percent of retail traders lose money over a twelve-month period. That number is not a coincidence, and it is not bad luck. It is the predictable result of how retail traders approach the market.

If you are on the wrong side of that statistic, the good news is that the fix is not a secret indicator or a smarter strategy. The fix is a process.

## Reason one: over-leverage

Most new traders do not start with a blown account because their analysis was wrong. They blow up because a single trade was too big to survive.

The math is simple. If you risk 20 percent of your account on one trade and you are wrong twice in a row, you are down roughly 36 percent. You now need a 56 percent recovery just to get back to even. That is before you have even started making money.

Professional desks do not trade this way. They risk a fraction of a percent per idea, so that being wrong five times in a row is inconvenient, not fatal.

## Reason two: strategy hopping

The second killer is switching systems after every losing streak. A trader tries a moving average crossover, loses four times, reads about supply and demand on YouTube, switches, loses three more times, switches again.

The problem is not the strategy. The problem is that no approach has been given a long enough run to produce a statistically meaningful sample. You cannot judge an edge from ten trades. You need fifty, preferably a hundred.

**The fix is to pick one methodology and commit to it for at least one full quarter of consistent application** before you allow yourself to evaluate whether it works.

## Reason three: no journal, no review

If a trader cannot tell you why their last five trades worked and why the next five did not, they are not trading. They are gambling with charts.

A trading journal does three things:

- Forces you to articulate your setup before you take it, which catches most impulsive trades.
- Creates a record you can actually review, so patterns become visible.
- Turns emotional losses into data, which is easier to look at on a Sunday afternoon than in the moment.

You do not need software for this. A Google Sheet is fine. What matters is that every trade has a written reason, a planned risk, and a post-trade note.

## How to flip the odds

The traders who survive the first year share three habits, and none of them are about finding a better entry signal:

1. They risk a fixed, small percentage per trade, usually one percent or less.
2. They trade one methodology long enough to gather real evidence on it.
3. They journal every trade and review the journal weekly.

None of that is exciting. It does not make for a good social media post. But it is the actual difference between the 10 percent who last and the 90 percent who do not.

The market does not pay for being clever. It pays for being consistent.
