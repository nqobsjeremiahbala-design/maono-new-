Position sizing is the most important skill in trading, and the one most people never learn properly.

Here is the whole concept on one page, with numbers you can actually use.

## The goal

Every trade should risk the **same amount of money**, regardless of the stop loss size. If you risk R100 per trade, you risk R100 whether your stop is 5 pips away or 50.

The lot size changes. The risk does not.

## The formula

Risk amount = Account balance × Risk percentage

Lot size = Risk amount ÷ (Stop loss in pips × Pip value)

That is it. Three numbers. Every trading platform has a built-in calculator for the second line, so you do not have to memorise pip values for every pair.

## Worked example

Let us say:

- Account: R20,000
- Risk per trade: 1% → R200
- Stop loss: 30 pips
- Pair: EURUSD, standard account

For EURUSD, one standard lot moves by R170 per pip (roughly, depending on exchange rate).

Position size = R200 ÷ (30 × R170) = 0.039 lots, so about 0.04 standard lots, or 0.4 mini lots.

If you punch this into your broker, the trade will lose exactly R204 if it hits the stop. That is the number you wanted.

## Why this matters more than your strategy

Two traders use the same strategy. Both win 55% of trades with a 1:1.5 risk-to-reward.

Trader A risks 1% per trade. After 100 trades, account is up about 20%. Reasonable.

Trader B eyeballs position size and sometimes risks 1%, sometimes 3%, sometimes 5% when they are feeling confident. After 100 trades, account is down 12%. Same strategy. Different outcome.

The strategy did not fail. The sizing failed.

## Three rules

- **Decide your risk percent before opening the chart.** Not after seeing the setup.
- **Never size up a position because you are "really sure."** Confidence is not a risk parameter.
- **If you cannot enter the trade at the correct size, skip it.** Do not reduce the stop to fit a bigger position. The stop is defined by the structure, not by your account.

## Try it

Calculate your next three trades using the formula above. Write down the risk amount beforehand. Compare it to what you actually lost when a trade stopped out.

If the two numbers match, your sizing works. If they do not, your sizing is broken and it does not matter what your strategy is.
