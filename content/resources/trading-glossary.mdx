Most trading glossaries are copy-pasted from textbooks. This one is the thirty terms we use every day inside the community, explained the way we would explain them to a new member.

## The basics

- **Pip** — the smallest standard price movement. On EURUSD, a pip is the fourth decimal. 1.1000 → 1.1001 is one pip.
- **Lot** — the unit of a position. One standard lot is 100,000 units of the base currency. Mini lot is 10,000. Micro lot is 1,000.
- **Leverage** — how much bigger your position is than your account. 1:100 leverage means R1,000 in your account controls R100,000 of currency. Amplifies both wins and losses.
- **Spread** — the gap between buy and sell price. This is what your broker charges you on every trade.
- **Long** — a buy position. You profit if price goes up.
- **Short** — a sell position. You profit if price goes down.

## Charting

- **Candlestick** — a single price window showing open, high, low, and close.
- **Timeframe** — how much time one candle represents. M5 = five minutes. H1 = one hour. D1 = one day.
- **Higher timeframe (HTF)** — daily and up. Used for context.
- **Lower timeframe (LTF)** — 1-hour and below. Used for entries.
- **Structure** — the pattern of highs and lows on a chart. Higher highs and higher lows = uptrend. Lower highs and lower lows = downtrend.

## Levels

- **Support** — a zone where price has historically stopped falling.
- **Resistance** — a zone where price has historically stopped rising.
- **Supply zone** — a wider version of resistance, showing where sellers previously dominated.
- **Demand zone** — a wider version of support, showing where buyers previously dominated.
- **Liquidity** — a cluster of stop losses or pending orders. Institutional traders hunt these.

## Trade mechanics

- **Stop loss** — the price at which you accept the trade is wrong and close it.
- **Take profit** — the price at which you accept the trade worked and close it.
- **Risk-to-reward (RR)** — the ratio of what you stand to lose vs. what you stand to make. 1:2 means risking R100 to make R200.
- **Breakeven** — moving your stop to your entry price, so the trade cannot lose.

## Psychology

- **Overtrading** — taking trades that do not meet your plan. Usually driven by revenge, boredom, or FOMO.
- **FOMO** — fear of missing out. The feeling that makes you chase price without a setup.
- **Revenge trading** — trying to immediately win back what you just lost. Almost always makes it worse.
- **Tilt** — being in an emotional state where your decisions no longer follow your plan.
- **Discipline** — the only real edge. Everything else is technique.

## Market types

- **Trend** — price moving consistently in one direction, with higher highs and higher lows, or the inverse.
- **Range** — price oscillating between two horizontal levels, with no clear directional bias.
- **Breakout** — price moving decisively beyond a level it had previously respected.
- **Retest** — price returning to a broken level to confirm it as new support or resistance.

## News and sessions

- **Session** — the period when a particular financial centre is most active. London, New York, and Tokyo are the main three.
- **NFP** — Non-Farm Payrolls. US jobs data released on the first Friday of each month. Usually volatile.
- **CPI** — Consumer Price Index. Inflation data. Major market mover.
- **Rate decision** — when a central bank announces whether it is raising, holding, or lowering interest rates.

## One more thing
