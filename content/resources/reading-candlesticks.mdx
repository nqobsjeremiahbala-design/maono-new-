Most new traders learn the four prices on a candle and stop there. A candle is more useful than that. It is a story about who was in control during that window of time.

## The four prices, quickly

Every candle shows:

- **Open** — where price started
- **High** — the highest it reached
- **Low** — the lowest it reached
- **Close** — where it finished

Bullish candles close above their open. Bearish candles close below. This is the easy part.

## What the body is saying

The **body** is the distance between open and close. It tells you how decisive the move was.

- **Large body, small wicks** — one side dominated the entire window. Strong intent.
- **Small body, long wicks** — both sides fought, and nobody closed the deal. Indecision.
- **Small body near a level** — often a better signal than a big body, especially at a key support or resistance.

A huge green candle is not always bullish. If it closes into a major resistance, it often marks the point where sellers step in.

## What the wicks are saying

Wicks are rejections. They show where price went but could not stay.

- A long upper wick means price pushed up, then got sold back down. Supply stepped in.
- A long lower wick means price pushed down, then got bought back up. Demand stepped in.

Wicks at key levels are one of the clearest signals of institutional participation. If a 4-hour candle prints a long lower wick into a daily support, that is not noise — that is someone buying.

## Context is everything

A bullish engulfing candle in the middle of a range is worth nothing. The same candle at the bottom of a swing, into a level you had already marked, is a potential trade.

Never read a candle in isolation. Always ask:

- Where is price in the bigger picture?
- What level is this candle forming at?
- What has the last three candles been doing?

The candle is the trigger. The level is the reason.
